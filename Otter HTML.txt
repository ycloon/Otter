        <h2>What is a Sea Otter?</h2>
        <p>Sea otters (<em>Enhydra lutris</em>) are marine mammals native to the coasts of the northern and eastern North Pacific Ocean. Unlike other marine mammals, they rely heavily on their exceptionally dense fur rather than blubber to stay warm in cold ocean waters.</p>
    </section>

    <!-- Adaptations Grid -->
    <section id="adaptations" class="light-bg">
        <div class="container section">
            <h2>Key Physical Adaptations</h2>
            <div class="grid">
                <div class="card">
                    <h3>Super-Dense Fur</h3>
                    <p>They possess up to 1 million hairs per square inch, trapping a layer of air to keep water away from their skin.</p>
                </div>
                <div class="card">
                    <h3>High Metabolism</h3>
                    <p>Otters must eat roughly 25% of their body weight daily to maintain their high internal body temperature.</p>
                </div>
                <div class="card">
                    <h3>Tool Users</h3>
                    <p>They are among the few mammals that use rocks to crack open hard-shelled prey like crabs, clams, and urchins.</p>
                </div>
            </div>
        </div>
    </section>

    <!-- Ecosystem Section -->
    <section id="ecosystem" class="container section">
        <h2>Keystone Species Role</h2>
        <p>Sea otters play a vital role in maintaining the balance of nearshore marine ecosystems. By eating sea urchins, they protect giant kelp forests from being overgrazed, which in turn helps capture atmospheric carbon and supports diverse marine life.</p>
