THE STORY

Built for two worlds.

Few mammals move between land and water with the confidence of an otter.

Otters belong to the Mustelidae family, a group that also includes weasels, badgers and ferrets. Their long bodies, muscular tails, sensitive whiskers and powerful swimming abilities make them highly successful aquatic hunters.

Some species occupy freshwater rivers and lakes while sea otters spend almost their entire lives along marine coastlines.

Their intelligence is equally extraordinary. Sea otters are among the few mammals known to use tools, often using stones to open shellfish.

SURVIVAL

Life at the surface

For an otter, water is not simply a habitat. It is a hunting ground, a refuge and sometimes even a bed.

INTELLIGENCE

The animal that carries its own toolbox

Sea otters sometimes dive to the seafloor, collect hard-shelled prey and return to the surface carrying a stone.

Floating on their backs, they can place the rock on their chest like an anvil and strike clams, mussels or other prey against it.

Tool use is one of the clearest examples of the sea otter's remarkable intelligence.

KEYSTONE SPECIES

One predator.
An entire ecosystem.

Sea otters influence far more than the animals they eat. By controlling sea urchins, they can help protect kelp forests, which provide shelter and food for countless marine organisms.

How well do you know otters?

Click each question to reveal the answer.
Why do sea otters spend so much time grooming?

Grooming helps maintain the insulating layer of air trapped inside their dense fur. A well-maintained coat is essential for staying warm in cold water.

Why are sea otters important to kelp forests?

Sea otters eat animals such as sea urchins. When urchin populations are controlled, kelp forests have a better chance of surviving and expanding.

Are all otters marine animals?

No. Most otter species depend heavily on freshwater environments such as rivers, wetlands and lakes. Sea otters are especially adapted for marine life.

Why are their whiskers important?

Otter whiskers are highly sensitive and can detect changes in water movement, which helps when searching for prey.

Do otters really use rocks?

Yes. Sea otters are famous for using stones to crack open hard-shelled prey such as clams and mussels.

CONSERVATION

Their future depends on ours.

Otters face threats including habitat loss, pollution, fishing activity, declining prey populations and historical hunting.

Protecting wetlands, rivers, coastal habitats and healthy marine ecosystems is essential not only for otters, but for the countless species that share their world.
